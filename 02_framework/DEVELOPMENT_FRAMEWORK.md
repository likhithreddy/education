# Twelve-Dimension Child Capability Framework
**Not a validated psychometric instrument.** Use for planning and reflection, not diagnosis, ranking, selection or promises of perfection.

| Domain | Age 9–12 practice | Observable evidence | Boundary |
|---|---|---|---|
| Emotional regulation | name feelings, recover after frustration | uses coping strategy with support | never diagnose |
| Social relationships | listening, collaboration, conflict repair | takes turns and negotiates | respect neurodiversity |
| Financial judgment | plan a small budget, needs/wants | explains tradeoffs | no real investment advice |
| Critical thinking | ask for evidence, compare options | revises decision after feedback | no IQ claims |
| Physical well-being | movement, hydration, safety | chooses safe movement | adapt for disability |
| Practical independence | food hygiene, organizing tasks | completes supervised practical task | no hazardous tools |
| Creativity | design and iterate a prototype | produces and revises an idea | no aesthetic ranking |
| Communication | explain, listen, present | communicates to an audience | offer nonverbal alternatives |
| Character and ethics | honesty, care, responsibility | discusses ethical tradeoffs | avoid moral shaming |
| Digital/AI literacy | verify outputs, privacy, scams | identifies unreliable AI claim | no unmoderated child AI chat |
| Exploration and agency | try roles and reflect on interests | describes preferences | do not force career labels |
| Citizenship/environment | community needs, stewardship | completes small service action | no political indoctrination |

## Progression philosophy
Ages 3–6: play, attachment, language, exploration; ages 7–10: routines, cooperation, simple decisions; ages 11–14: complex teamwork, digital safety, practical reasoning; ages 15–18: adult readiness, consent, finances, civic participation and self-directed projects. All require developmental specialist review and accessible adaptations.

## Portfolio model
Collect a small number of artifacts: child-selected photo of work (optional consent), reflection, mentor observation, guardian feedback. Use descriptive statements: 'explained two ways to solve a problem', not 'social IQ 87/100'.

## Quality expansion — actionable protocols

## Educational design rules
A domain is an organizing lens; skills must be defined as behaviors. Example: communication is not “confidence” as a personal label but asking a clarifying question, restating a peer's request, and describing a decision. Each challenge must integrate at least three dimensions with role choice, child autonomy, reflection and feasible observation.

### Twelve-domain integration
Emotional awareness; relationships; financial decisions; reasoning; physical literacy; practical independence; making and creativity; communication; character/ethics; digital and AI judgment; agency and exploration; civic/environmental responsibility.

### Per-activity contract
Every planned experience must document: child-relevant scenario; preconditions; facilitator demonstration; authentic choices; inclusive adaptations; prompts; learning evidence; safety controls; optional family follow-through; explicit stop condition. Record whether the child enjoyed it, not only the desired skill.

### Reviewer challenge
What would make this task coercive, stigmatizing, overly complex, developmentally inappropriate or inaccessible? What alternate mode produces equivalent participation? What evidence could disprove our theory of benefit?

### Guardrails
Avoid rigid developmental rank or fixed personality labels, and do not equate high achievement with high income, obedience or introversion/extroversion. Invite independent specialist revision.
