# Positioning and responsible claims
**Position:** experiential life-readiness enrichment for children, not school replacement, therapy or certification.
**Permitted language:** 'designed to help children practice', 'pilot', 'hands-on experiences', 'parent feedback collected'.
**Not permitted without credible evidence:** 'proven to increase IQ', 'guaranteed confidence', 'makes children future-proof', 'prevents mental health problems', 'world's first', 'scientifically validated outcomes'.
**Child privacy:** no names, identifiable photos, testimonials or work products in marketing without specific informed guardian permission and appropriate child assent.
**AI claims:** AI may support internal lesson planning; do not imply child-facing AI tutoring if absent.
**Review:** founder and safeguarding lead approve all public claims before release.

## Implementation expansion (requires review)

## Claims substantiation rules
**Allowed with honest framing:** “proposed,” “designed to give practice in budgeting and teamwork,” “facilitator-led,” “no grades,” “optional family activities,” provided design and delivery facts are true.

**Prohibited without strong independent evidence:** “scientifically proven to develop intelligence,” “guaranteed future-ready,” “our method replaces school,” “prevents mental health issues,” “uniquely the only program in India,” or numerical gains without a valid study.

**Testimonials:** obtain voluntary, informed permission and clearly identify pilot context. Avoid editing quotes into stronger developmental claims. Never use child images to imply medical or psychological outcomes.

**Competitive messaging:** name factual alternative features only when recently verified. Do not invent poor safety standards or quality at competitor institutions.

**Review workflow:** every campaign receives evidence reference, claim risk level, child safeguarding review for imagery and founder approval. Archive previous wording and complaints.

## Expanded operating instructions

## Claim review rules
**Low risk, if truthful:** “Designed for hands-on practice in teamwork and financial decisions”; “12-week proposed pilot”; “non-ranking feedback”; “optional family activities.”

**Do not publish without strong proof:** “guaranteed future ready,” “our method increases IQ,” “prevents mental health problems,” “replaces school,” “the only program in India,” or numerical progress from unvalidated rubrics.

For each proposed marketing statement record claim text, evidence link and date, reviewer, risk level, revised approved wording and validity period. Testimonials should preserve actual meaning and require separate consent; no pressure on guardians or children.

Comparisons must be factual, recent, and avoid unsourced negative statements about named competitors. No fear-based AI messaging or shame-based performance charts.
