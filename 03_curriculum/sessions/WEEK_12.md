# Week 12 — Showcase and reflection
**Age:** 9–12 (pilot); **Duration:** 180 minutes; **Status:** facilitator-ready draft, pending expert and safety approval.

## Learning intent
Share learning without ranking. Domains: All domains. Children may opt out of public speaking, choose a quiet activity, and request help without penalty.

## Preparation (before arrival)
- Confirm guardian permissions, allergies, accommodations, staff ratios and emergency contacts with designated safeguarding lead.
- Inspect room, accessible toilets, exits and materials; remove sharp/hot/unsafe objects.
- Prepare 4 groups of up to 5 with adult supervision; adjust group sizes to actual approved ratios.
- Print child-friendly visual schedule, scenario cards, reflection sheets and activity instructions.

## Detailed facilitator sequence
| Time | Facilitation | Evidence / check |
|---|---|---|
| 00:00–00:15 | Welcome; child choice and feelings check-in; review group agreement | Participation without forced disclosure |
| 00:15–00:35 | Warm-up cooperative game with alternative seated option | Peer interaction and access |
| 00:35–01:00 | Model the challenge and demonstrate one example | Children can restate instructions |
| 01:00–01:20 | Teams plan and assign voluntary roles | Planning notes |
| 01:20–01:35 | Supervised water, toilet and movement break | Welfare check |
| 01:35–02:10 | Hands-on task: optional demonstration and private child-led portfolio review | Work sample / observation |
| 02:10–02:20 | Introduce safe complication or new constraint | Adaptive response |
| 02:20–02:40 | Revise, test or rehearse in groups | Evidence of iteration |
| 02:40–02:50 | Private or voluntary share-out; acknowledge different approaches | Child reflection |
| 02:50–03:00 | Cleanup and documented authorized pickup | Handover log |

## Materials (quantities for 20 children; confirm per venue)
- 20 reflection cards, 20 pencils, 5 large sheets, 5 sets of washable markers, 1 visual timer, 1 first-aid kit managed by trained adult.
- Theme-specific: Optional demonstration and private child-led portfolio review. Prepare five equivalent station kits, printed prompt cards and one accessible alternative per station.
- No identifiable child photos, voice recordings or AI account access without separate valid consent and controls.

## Inclusive adaptations
Use large-print instructions, quiet space, visual choices, flexible movement, nonverbal responses and adjustable roles. Never equate confidence with loudness or speed. Offer sensory-safe alternatives.

## Safety and escalation
Stop immediately for injury, distress, bullying, missing child, allergic symptoms or unsafe equipment. Follow approved emergency plan and incident reporting SOP. Week 5 requires allergy-specific professional review; week 6 is awareness only, not medical certification; week 9 uses fictional data and pre-reviewed offline examples.

## Observable outcomes (non-ranking)
Record a dated, factual example of (1) child choice/agency, (2) collaborative problem-solving, and (3) reflection. Use 'not observed / emerging / demonstrated with support / demonstrated independently' only when appropriate; never label a child's personality.

## Parent connection
Offer an optional 10–15 minute home conversation or low-cost task related to **showcase and reflection**; no homework grade, required spending or personal financial disclosure.

## Facilitator debrief
What engaged children? Which instructions failed? What accommodations were used? Any safety concerns? What should change next week? Log changes before repeating session.

## Facilitator extension v2 — Capstone showcase and closure

### Concrete demonstrations and scenario prompts
1. **Scenario 1:** Set up low-pressure stations where children voluntarily explain projects to approved visitors. Ask children: “What information would change your decision?”, “Whose perspective is missing?”, and “How can we test this safely?”
2. **Scenario 2:** Ask teams to demonstrate one decision, one iteration, one kind act and one unresolved question. Ask children: “What information would change your decision?”, “Whose perspective is missing?”, and “How can we test this safely?”
3. **Scenario 3:** Invite guardian feedback separately and close with an anonymous child comfort/engagement check. Ask children: “What information would change your decision?”, “Whose perspective is missing?”, and “How can we test this safely?”

### 180-minute delivery protocol
- **0–15 arrival:** check-in, quiet space and voluntary introductions.
- **15–35 connection:** model target behavior twice including a mistake and respectful correction.
- **35–60 guided demonstration:** elicit alternatives rather than one 'correct personality'.
- **60–70 supervised break:** confirm every child's location and accessibility.
- **70–110 main challenge:** supervised groups. At minutes 80, 95 and 105 observe choices rather than inferred traits.
- **110–125 restorative break:** offer supervised unstructured choice.
- **125–155 variation:** introduce a constraint and let children adapt or opt out.
- **155–175 reflection:** oral, drawn or written reflection; discuss uncertainty and support.
- **175–180 handover:** authorized pickup and confidential incident log.

### Evidence and evaluation
Use observed behaviors plus child-selected artifacts; show only consented materials. Use activity-specific descriptions: Not observed; Attempted with support; Demonstrated with support; Demonstrated independently. Record prompts and exact actions, never a permanent child label. Use pseudonymous records.

### Safeguarding stop rule
Verify visitor authorization, photo permissions, pickup controls and emotional safety. Stop for distress, bullying, injury risk, unknown pickup, inadequate supervision or unaddressed access needs. Escalate confidentially.

### Quality checks
Rehearse scenarios, inspect materials, assign staff roles, prepare opt-out alternatives. Afterward document prompts that worked, observations, and one improvement. Independent review is required before child-facing delivery.
