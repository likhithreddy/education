# Capstone — community improvement studio
**Weeks 10–12.** Children choose from fictional or safely observed issues (litter, accessible play, water conservation, inclusive signage). No unsupervised neighborhood fieldwork.
1. Define a problem in one sentence without naming affected individuals.
2. Interview *fictional personas* or use pre-approved anonymized examples.
3. Brainstorm three solutions; select one with a reason.
4. Create low-risk mock-up or poster; test with peers.
5. Reflect: what changed, what did we learn, who might be affected?
6. Offer an optional showcase; no competition or ranking.
**Artifacts:** challenge canvas, idea sketches, prototype photo only if separately consented (otherwise paper notes), group reflection, individual choice reflection.
**Review:** mentors record observed behaviors, not talent labels. Guardian feedback is voluntary.

## Capstone facilitation contract
Children select from at least three safe options: a design prototype, a fictional community-service planning exercise, or a private portfolio narrative. No public performance requirement. Groups may rotate planner, builder, communicator, verifier and wellbeing roles.

**Week 11:** define an issue and beneficiaries; state assumptions; propose resources; select evidence; get a facilitator safety sign-off before making. **Week 12:** demonstrate only consented work, describe one iteration, one uncertainty and one contribution.

**Rubric:** quality of explanation and reflection *in this activity*; meaningful child choice; listening and safe handling; capacity to revise after feedback. Avoid competitive ranking, personality judgments or pressure to show photographs.

**Showcase control:** approved visitor roster, staff present, authorized pickup, no external photography without separate permission, child opt-out route and quiet space. Collect anonymous child experience feedback after visitors leave.
