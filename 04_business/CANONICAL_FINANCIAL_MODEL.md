# Canonical Financial Model and Assumption Register — v0.1

**Purpose:** remove ambiguity between early illustrative budgets. This is a decision-support model, not an actual budget or forecast. All prices, hours, staffing and registrations must be verified before any commercial commitment.

## One model of record

For calculations in this version, use **₹110,000 per cohort of fixed direct cost** and **₹1,400 per enrolled child variable direct cost**, both explicitly **illustrative**. The earlier simplified ₹135,000 aggregate expense assumption is superseded for scenario comparison; it was never a quotation.

At 20 participants, gross price ₹9,000:
- Revenue = 20 × ₹9,000 = **₹180,000**.
- Direct cost = ₹110,000 + (20 × ₹1,400) = **₹138,000**.
- Contribution before overhead, income tax, launch capex and financing = **₹42,000**, or 23.33% of illustrative gross revenue.
- Break-even on direct cost alone is ceiling(₹110,000 / (₹9,000 - ₹1,400)) = **15 participants**. This is valid **only if** direct fixed costs and child capacity remain unchanged and fees are fully realized.

## Formula definitions
Let N = enrolled and collected participants; P = average realized program revenue per participant after promotions and refunds; F = fixed direct expenses; V = direct variable expense per participant; CAC = acquisition cost per new paying family; O = allocated central overhead and one-time costs as applicable.

Revenue = N × P.
Direct cost = F + N × V.
Contribution = N × (P - V) - F.
Direct break-even enrollments = ceil(F / (P - V)), where P > V.
Operating result = contribution - sales/marketing overhead - central overhead - amortized setup where applicable.
Actual cash position must separately consider deposit dates, refunds, prepayments, tax deposits and vendor settlement timing.

## Scenario analysis
Low: 10 participants at ₹6,000; base: 20 at ₹9,000; high: 24 at ₹12,000. The high case is **not** operable unless the venue, qualified staffing and activity-specific child-to-adult ratio have been approved for 24.

Run at least four sensitivity tests: (a) 25% higher fixed direct cost, (b) 50% higher variable cost, (c) 20% lower realized fee after refunds/discounts, (d) a launch delayed by one month. When external quotations arrive, replace each assumption and rerun every scenario.

## Financial red flags
- Do not count nonrefundable deposits as profit before the service/refund obligations are clear.
- Do not compare one-off camps directly with 12-week subscriptions without normalizing scope, supervision, materials and hours.
- Do not increase cohort size beyond safe capacity to improve contribution.
- Show tax and GST treatment separately after chartered accountant review.
- Charge acquisition costs and founder time transparently in an economic assessment even if cash-out is zero.
- Maintain adequate cash to refund all participants if a serious safety or legal blocker forces cancellation.
