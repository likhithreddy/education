# Project Charter
## Problem
Families often combine schooling with fragmented extracurricular offerings; it is unclear whether a coordinated life-capability experience can create more value at acceptable cost.
## Vision
An adaptive ecosystem of age-appropriate real-world experiences, family support and human mentorship, with AI assisting planning and reflection—not substituting for care or judgment.
## Target customer and beneficiary
Paying customer: parent/guardian of child aged 9–12 in Hyderabad. Beneficiary: child. Pilot excludes claims about serving every age or disability without appropriate accommodations.
## Value hypothesis
A coherent multi-domain experiential journey may be more convenient and meaningful than independently coordinating numerous classes.
## Principles
Child-first; consent and assent; play and autonomy; inclusive design; privacy by default; no competitive scoring; qualified supervision; no speculative AI-development claims; academic learning remains valuable.
## Pilot scope
12 weekly 3-hour facilitated sessions; 20 children maximum subject to safety staffing and space; 12 domain framework; 1 capstone; guardian feedback.
## Out of scope
School replacement, accredited education, childcare/daycare, therapy, diagnosis, live public selling by children, unsupervised offsite travel, unreviewed AI chat with minors.
## Decision rights
Founder: budget, venue, staffing contracts, pricing, go/no-go. Safeguarding lead: authority to stop unsafe activities. Curriculum specialist: developmental suitability. AI: research and drafts only.
## Success decision
Proceed only if parents demonstrate demand, children report safe/enjoyable experience, facilitators can deliver consistently, safeguarding gates pass, and economics are credible. Avoid fabricated numerical impact claims.

## Quality expansion — implementation requirements

## Investment-grade strategic thesis

This venture tests whether a safe, enjoyable, recurring set of experiential challenges can support capabilities beyond routine academic instruction while remaining affordable and operationally sustainable. The intended customer is a guardian; the child is an autonomous participant whose interest and safety override conversion objectives.

**Three hypotheses must be independently falsifiable:** (H1) a specific Hyderabad customer segment has an unmet need that current substitutes do not meet; (H2) participating children voluntarily return and demonstrate practical behaviors in the designed setting; (H3) the service can be delivered safely, inclusively and with defensible unit economics. Neither AI adoption nor a changing job market proves demand.

**Initial boundaries:** one optional 12-week cohort, ages 9–12, three hours per week; not formal school replacement, clinical diagnosis, psychological profiling, employment preparation, or unsupervised AI companionship.

**Stakeholder responsibilities:** Founder signs investments and go/no-go; child-development specialist reviews curriculum; safeguarding lead has independent stop authority; facilitators manage child-facing sessions; technical lead handles restricted data systems; AI produces drafts and analyses, not legal authorization.

**Strategic metrics:** paid enrollment conversion, child assent and attendance, parent feedback response, documented enjoyment, safety incident outcomes, direct cost per attended child-hour, refund rate, contribution after variable and direct fixed costs. Register thresholds *before* marketing results are seen.

**Evidence standard:** each gate includes an accountable reviewer, dated primary records, alternatives considered, dissent/uncertainty and explicit approval. Drafts are not approvals.

**Ethical constraints:** protect childhood, allow free play, opt-outs, privacy and non-ranking. Avoid fear-based selling (“AI will replace your child”) or claims of guaranteed future success.
