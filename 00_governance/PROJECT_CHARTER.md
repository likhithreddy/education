# Project Charter
## Problem
Families often combine schooling with fragmented extracurricular offerings; it is unclear whether a coordinated life-capability experience can create more value at acceptable cost.
## Vision
An adaptive ecosystem of age-appropriate real-world experiences, family support and human mentorship, with AI assisting planning and reflection—not substituting for care or judgment.
## Target customer and beneficiary
Paying customer: parent/guardian of child aged 9–12 in Hyderabad. Beneficiary: child. Pilot excludes claims about serving every age or disability without appropriate accommodations.
## Value hypothesis
A coherent multi-domain experiential journey may be more convenient and meaningful than independently coordinating numerous classes.
## Principles
Child-first; consent and assent; play and autonomy; inclusive design; privacy by default; no competitive scoring; qualified supervision; no speculative AI-development claims; academic learning remains valuable.
## Pilot scope
12 weekly 3-hour facilitated sessions; 20 children maximum subject to safety staffing and space; 12 domain framework; 1 capstone; guardian feedback.
## Out of scope
School replacement, accredited education, childcare/daycare, therapy, diagnosis, live public selling by children, unsupervised offsite travel, unreviewed AI chat with minors.
## Decision rights
Founder: budget, venue, staffing contracts, pricing, go/no-go. Safeguarding lead: authority to stop unsafe activities. Curriculum specialist: developmental suitability. AI: research and drafts only.
## Success decision
Proceed only if parents demonstrate demand, children report safe/enjoyable experience, facilitators can deliver consistently, safeguarding gates pass, and economics are credible. Avoid fabricated numerical impact claims.
